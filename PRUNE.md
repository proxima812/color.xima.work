rationale, kept so the reasoning is reviewable rather than buried in a diff.

### 3. `mesh-nova-*` family (60)
Generated by a mechanical hue rotation of about +22 degrees per card, with no colour judgement
applied. Each card mixed three widely-separated hues over a fourth — green with magenta, yellow
with indigo — which landed as washed-out pastel soup, and every card was interchangeable with its
neighbours. They were also the only mesh cards without a border, so they read as unfinished in the
grid. The names were arbitrary: `onyx-petal` was peach, `frost-mist` was green, `umbra-aura` cream.

### 4. Mislabelled `noise-overlay-*` cards (18)
These contained no noise. They were `repeating-linear-gradient` diagonal stripes over an HSL
rainbow ramp — duplicating the `repeating` category while filed under `noise` — and the underlying
ramps swept 120 to 200 degrees of hue. `noise-overlay-duskgrain` was additionally broken: its
second stop was `12px 12px`, a zero-width band, so it rendered as a flat white wash with no stripes.

## Renamed — 66 entries

An off-by-one in whatever generated the `linear`, `repeating`, `transparent`, `noise`, `conic`,
`multi-stop`, `femme` and `masc` families left a long tail of cards named after a colour they did
not contain, which broke search: a query for "mint" returned violet.

Each surviving card was rendered to a pixel grid and its dominant hue measured, then any colour
word in the name more than 50 degrees away from the measured hue was replaced with the true one.
Names within 50 degrees were left alone, since those are judgement calls rather than errors, and
material compounds like `rose-gold` were exempted. Pale tints too desaturated to name confidently
were skipped.

## Note on URLs

Detail pages are keyed by slug, so the 162 removals and 66 renames retire 228 URLs. Favourites are
stored by slug in `localStorage` and will silently drop any entry pointing at a retired slug. If
the live site has meaningful inbound links or indexing, add redirects from the old slugs before
deploying.
